## Factors that determine the cost of a website

Before throwing numbers at you, you need to understand what makes a website cost more or less. Because it's not the same as buying a car or buying a website, but the analogy works: a Renault Sandero and a Mercedes-Benz do the same thing (take you from point A to point B), but the price, experience, and result are completely different.

### 1. Functional complexity

This is the most determining factor. It's not the same a landing page with a contact form as a marketplace with payment gateway, seller panel, review system, and real-time chat.

Each additional functionality implies:
- More development hours
- More quality testing
- More potential failure points
- More future maintenance

### 2. Custom design vs template

A custom design made from scratch can take between 40 and 120 hours of a professional UI/UX designer. A premium template from ThemeForest costs between $30 and $80 and is adapted in 8 to 16 hours. The price difference is abysmal, and so is the originality and fit to your brand.

### 3. Technology used

It's not the same a WordPress with Elementor as a site developed with Next.js, Astro, or React with a backend in Node.js or Laravel. Modern technologies offer better performance, security, and scalability, but require more specialized and therefore more expensive developers.

### 4. Content and SEO

If you need texts written, images optimized, metadata configured, schema markup implemented, and keyword research done, the cost goes up. Quality content isn't improvised, and good SEO is the difference between appearing on Google or being invisible.

### 5. Integrations with third parties

Do you need to connect your site with a CRM like HubSpot? With a payment gateway like Wompi, Mercado Pago, or Stripe? With your inventory system? With email marketing tools like Mailchimp? Each integration adds complexity and cost.

### 6. Delivery deadlines

An urgent project you need in 2 weeks costs more than one with a reasonable timeline of 6 to 8 weeks. Urgency implies exclusive dedication and overtime.

### 7. Provider location

A developer or agency in Bogotá doesn't charge the same as one in a small municipality. Cost of living, demand, and specialization vary significantly between regions. We'll see this in detail later.

## Hidden costs nobody tells you about

These are costs that appear after launch and most quotes don't include:

1. **Professional product photography**: If you sell physical products, catalog photos taken with a phone don't convert. A professional photo session in Bogotá for 50 products with white background costs between **$800,000 and $2,500,000 COP**.

2. **Product description copywriting**: Each SEO-optimized product sheet with persuasive descriptions can cost between **$15,000 and $40,000 COP per product** if you hire a specialized e-commerce copywriter. For 100 products, that's $1.5 to $4 million additional.

3. **Legal compliance and data protection**: In Colombia, every website that collects personal data must comply with Law 1581 on Data Protection. You need a privacy policy drafted by a lawyer (**$300,000–$600,000 COP**), terms and conditions (**$400,000–$800,000 COP**), and a changes and returns policy (**$200,000–$400,000 COP**).

4. **Advertising to generate traffic**: A website without traffic is a digital brochure nobody sees. You need budget for Google Ads (minimum **$1,000,000/month**), Meta Ads (minimum **$800,000/month**), and if you delegate it, SEO and content marketing (**$1,500,000–$4,000,000/month**).

The problem with cheap websites is that they don't generate results. They exist, but they don't sell. They don't appear on Google. They don't capture leads. They don't convert visitors into customers. A bad website is not a saving — it's a wasted expense.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do some developers charge $500,000 and others charge $15,000,000 for a website?

Because they're not selling the same thing. A $500,000 site is usually a template adapted in 2 hours with no customization, no SEO, no optimization. A $15,000,000 site involves custom design, advanced development, integrations, content strategy, and ongoing support. Compare what each proposal includes, not just the final number.

### Is it better to hire a freelancer or an agency?

It depends. For specific, well-defined projects, a senior freelancer offers better value for money. For complex projects that require multiple specialists working simultaneously (designer, developer, SEO specialist, copywriter), an agency has the structure to coordinate them. I covered this in depth in [Freelance Developer vs Agency](/blog/freelance-developer-vs-agency-web-project).

### How long does it take to develop a website in Colombia?

A landing page: 1-2 weeks. A corporate site of 5-10 pages: 3-5 weeks. An e-commerce: 6-10 weeks. A custom web application: 3-6 months. These timelines assume the client delivers content (texts, images) on time. The biggest delay factor in web projects is usually waiting for the client's content.

### Can I pay in installments?

Most developers and agencies work with milestone payments: 40% at start, 30% at design approval, 30% at launch. Some accept monthly payments for large projects. I personally offer flexible payment schemes adapted to each project's cash flow.

### What if I don't like the final result?

This should be defined in the contract before starting. In my process, I present wireframes and design mockups before writing a single line of code. You approve the design, then development begins. This eliminates surprises at the end. Make sure your developer has a revision process before committing to the full project.

### Do I need to pay for hosting and domain separately?

Usually yes. Some developers include the first year of hosting and domain in the project price. From the second year, you must renew the domain (~$45,000-$80,000 COP/year) and hosting (~$150,000-$400,000 COP/year). I recommend that the domain always be in your name, not the developer's.
