## What type of website does your business need?

Not all businesses need the same type of website. Asking a developer for "a website" without more context is like asking an architect for "a house" without telling them how many people, what terrain, and what budget. The result will be anything but what you need.

These are the main types of websites for small businesses in Colombia:

### 1. Landing page

**What it is**: A single page designed to convert visitors into customers or leads. No complex navigation menu, no distractions, with one clear objective.

**Who it's ideal for**:
- Businesses that offer **a single flagship product or service**
- Entrepreneurs who are going to **invest in paid advertising** (Google Ads, Facebook Ads)
- Businesses that want to **capture leads** (emails, WhatsApp numbers) to nurture later
- Product launches, events, or temporary promotions

**Example**: A landing page to promote "Valentine's Day romantic dinners at your restaurant" with a direct reservation button via WhatsApp.

Learn more about [landing pages in Bogotá](/servicios/landing-page/bogota) and how they can help you convert more customers.

### 2. Corporate or institutional website

**What it is**: A site of 5 to 10 pages with full navigation. Generally includes: home, services, about us, portfolio, blog, and contact.

**Who it's ideal for**:
- Businesses that offer **multiple services or product lines**
- Companies that need to **generate trust and credibility** with customers who research thoroughly
- Businesses that want to **rank on Google** with different keywords
- B2B professional services competing for high-value contracts

**Example**: A law firm in Bogotá with separate pages for labor, commercial, criminal, and family law.

### 3. Online store (e-commerce)

**What it is**: A website with a product catalog, shopping cart, payment gateway, and inventory and order management.

**Who it's ideal for**:
- Physical stores that want to **sell online without depending on marketplaces**
- Entrepreneurs who sell **physical products** and want full margin without commissions from platforms like MercadoLibre
- Own brands that want to **control the shopping experience** from start to finish

**Example**: A Colombian clothing brand that sells its designs directly from its website, integrating Wompi or Mercado Pago as the payment gateway.

### 4. Catalog or brochure website

**What it is**: A simple site of 3 to 5 pages that works as an expanded digital business card. It shows who you are, what you offer, and how to contact you.

**Who it's ideal for**:
- Very small businesses that are **starting their digital presence**
- Independent professionals with limited budget
- Businesses that already have customers but need **digital validation** (so that when someone searches for them on Google, something professional shows up)

**Example**: An electrician in Medellín with his site showing his services, certifications, coverage area, and WhatsApp number.

### Which one do I need?

The answer depends on three factors:

1. **Your main objective**: sell online? generate leads? provide information so they contact you?
2. **Your initial budget**: do you have $1 million or $10 million to invest?
3. **Your 12-month growth plan**: are you going to scale the business or keep it as is?

## Examples by type of small business

So you don't stay in theory, here are concrete examples of four very common types of small businesses in Colombia. In each case I explain what functionalities the website needs and why.

### Restaurant or food business

**Case**: "Doña Ceci" restaurant in the Chapinero neighborhood, Bogotá. Traditional Colombian cuisine. 8 tables. Open Monday to Saturday from 12 p.m. to 9 p.m. They do their own deliveries.

**What their website needs**:

- **Updatable digital menu**: with real photos of the dishes, descriptions, and prices. No blurry scanned PDFs.
- **Direct WhatsApp button**: for reservations and deliveries. With a predefined message: "Hello, I want to place an order of...".
- **Location with embedded Google Maps**: so customers arrive without getting lost.
- **Visible opening hours**: at the top of all pages, not hidden in the footer.
- **Dish gallery**: because food enters through the eyes. Well-taken photos, not ones the waiter snapped with his phone in the dark kitchen.
- **Reviews section**: integrated with real Google reviews, not made-up testimonials.
- **Events or promotions**: if they have "Thursday bandeja paisa for $15,000", it should be highlighted.

**What it doesn't need**: its own payment gateway. Deliveries are better managed via WhatsApp or by linking to Rappi/Didi Food if they work with them.

**Estimated budget**: $800,000 to $1,800,000 COP for a complete landing page with all these functionalities. If you also want a reservation system with calendar, add $500,000 to $1,000,000 extra.

**Expected result**: A restaurant that previously depended 100% on foot traffic now receives between 15 and 30 additional weekly orders via the web. With an average ticket of $25,000, that represents between $1,500,000 and $3,000,000 of extra income per month. The investment pays for itself in the first month.

### Physical store or retail location

**Case**: "Variedades La Solución", a miscellaneous and stationery store in a residential neighborhood in Cali. They sell school supplies, cleaning items, decoration, and gifts.

**What their website needs**:

- **Product catalog by categories**: they don't need to sell online yet, but they do need to show what they have. A catalog with photos, prices, and availability.
- **"Where are we?" page**: with map, photo of the store facade (so customers recognize it), nearby bus stops, and neighborhood references.
- **Extended hours and special days**: "Open Sundays 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. during back-to-school season".
- **Contact form and WhatsApp**: to ask about availability of specific products.
- **Featured monthly offers**: updatable section with current promotions.
- **Integration with Google My Business**: to appear in Google's "Local Pack" (the 3 businesses that show up with a map in search results).

**What it doesn't need**: complete e-commerce yet. They can start with a catalog and enable online sales later when they validate demand.

**Estimated budget**: $1,200,000 to $2,500,000 COP for a 5 to 8 page catalog site with custom design.

**Expected result**: More traffic to the physical store. Customers from nearby neighborhoods who didn't know the store existed now find it on Google when they search for "stationery near me" or "where to buy school supplies in Cali". 20% to 40% increase in new customers during the first semester.

### Independent professional service

**Case**: Carlos, independent public accountant in Bucaramanga. He serves individuals and small businesses. He does tax returns, monthly accounting, and tax advisory.

**What his website needs**:

- **Detailed services page**: each service with its clear description, who it's for, what it includes, and what it does NOT include. This filters clients by itself.
- **Estimated fee calculator**: he doesn't need to give the exact price, but a range helps the client arrive prepared. Transparency generates trust.
- **"About me" section with real photo and credentials**: professional services are sold on trust. A professional photo, your education, your professional card, and years of experience make the difference versus another accountant who only has a Facebook profile.
- **Blog with useful articles**: "How to prepare your documents for the 2026 tax return", "Differences between individual and legal entity", "DIAN fines you can avoid". This positions you as an expert and attracts organic traffic from Google.
- **Verifiable testimonials**: with name, photo, and position of the client (with their permission). No "Juan P." that seems made up.
- **Initial consultation scheduling**: a Calendly or Google Calendar integration where the client can schedule a 15-minute call without endless WhatsApp chains.
- **Direct WhatsApp on all pages**: because in Colombia the final conversion almost always goes through WhatsApp.

**What it doesn't need**: overly creative design. For professional services, clarity and trust take priority over flashiness.

**Estimated budget**: $2,000,000 to $4,500,000 COP for a 6 to 10 page site with blog, scheduling system, and integrations.

**Expected result**: From 3-5 new clients per month by referral to 10-15 new clients per month combining referrals and organic traffic. For an accountant who charges $300,000 monthly per company, capturing 3 new clients per month via the website represents $900,000 in additional monthly income. It paid for itself in 5 months.

### Medical, dental, or wellness office

**Case**: "Sonrisa Vital" Dental Center, 2 dentists and 1 oral hygienist in the El Poblado neighborhood, Medellín.

**What their website needs**:

- **Services page with prices or ranges**: orthodontics, implants, whitening, cleaning, emergencies. If the price depends on the case, put "from $X". 80% of patients want to know how much it costs before scheduling.
- **Online appointment booking system**: with service selection, professional, and available time slot. Without having to call. Let the patient see real-time slots and book alone.
- **Profile of each professional**: with professional photo (white coat, smile, office in the background), university, specialty, years of experience, and a personal message.
- **Before/after case gallery**: with patient consent. Visible results sell more than a thousand words. A transformed smile convinces anyone that they are in the right place.
- **Frequently asked questions focused on objections**: "Does whitening hurt?", "How long does orthodontic treatment take?", "Do you have financing plans?", "Do you attend emergencies the same day?"
- **Map, available parking, and nearby public transportation**: logistics matter. If a patient doesn't know where to park, they may cancel the appointment.
- **Visible certifications and affiliations**: Health Secretariat, dental associations, updating courses. Health is trust.
- **Blog with educational content**: "How often to get a dental cleaning", "Implants vs bridges: differences", "How to care for braces on vacation". This attracts organic traffic from people searching for oral health information.

**Estimated budget**: $3,000,000 to $6,000,000 COP for a complete site with booking system, case gallery, and blog. The additional investment in the booking system (versus one without it) is recovered with the time savings of the receptionist who no longer spends 3 hours a day coordinating appointments by phone.

**Expected result**: 60% reduction in calls to schedule appointments (patients book themselves), 30% increase in new patients in 6 months, and 40% reduction in canceled appointments (automatic reminders by email and WhatsApp significantly reduce no-shows).

## Checklist: 15 questions you should ask a developer before hiring them

Hiring the wrong developer is the number one cause of websites that are never finished, that don't work on mobile, or that disappear after 3 months. This checklist will save you headaches, money, and time.

### About the project and methodology

- [ ] **1. "Can you show me 3 websites you have developed for businesses similar to mine?"** Not a generic portfolio. Ask for examples of businesses like yours: if you're a restaurant, show me websites of restaurants you've done. Check that they work well on mobile.

- [ ] **2. "What is your work process? Do you have a schedule with defined deliverables?"** If they say "I'll show you as I go", run. A professional has clear stages: research, wireframes, design, development, testing, launch. And gives you estimated dates for each one.

- [ ] **3. "Do you deliver the site with an administration panel so I can make basic changes?"** If the answer is no and every time you want to change a text or photo you have to pay them, your site will be a bottomless barrel. Ask for a CMS (WordPress, Strapi, Sanity) or at least editable files.

- [ ] **4. "Will the website be responsive? Can I see how it looks on mobile BEFORE approving?"** If the developer doesn't show you the mobile version during the design process, it's a red flag. In Colombia more than 85% of visits come from mobile.

### About technical aspects

- [ ] **5. "What technologies do you use and why are they appropriate for my business?"** You don't need to understand code, but you do need to know if they're offering you WordPress (more economical, easier to manage) or custom development (faster, more secure, more expensive). Ask them to explain the pros and cons in plain language.

- [ ] **6. "Does the site include basic SEO? What exactly?"** Basic SEO includes: title and description tags on each page, friendly URLs (no /?p=123), correct heading structure (H1, H2, H3), image optimization, XML sitemap, and connection with Google Search Console. If they don't know what Search Console is, they don't know SEO.

- [ ] **7. "Is loading speed optimized? What score do you expect on Google PageSpeed?"** A slow site is an invisible site. On mobile, Colombia has connections that aren't always fiber optic. The site must load in less than 3 seconds on an average data plan.

- [ ] **8. "Who buys the domain and hosting, you or me? Are they in my name?"** CRITICAL. The domain must be in your name and the hosting contracted by you. If the developer buys them in their name and one day disappears, you lost your domain and your site. It doesn't matter if they help you configure them, but the ownership must be yours.

- [ ] **9. "Does the site comply with the Colombian data protection law (Law 1581 of 2012)?"** If your site collects personal data (contact form, newsletter, appointments), it must have a privacy policy and data protection. It's not optional, it's law.

### About delivery and support

- [ ] **10. "Will I receive the source files of the site at the end of the project?"** If the answer is no, you're tied to that developer for life. The source files (code, designs) must be yours because you paid for them.

- [ ] **11. "Do you offer post-launch warranty? For how long and what does it cover?"** The normal thing is 30 to 60 days of warranty to fix errors that weren't detected during testing. Functional bugs, broken links, form problems. It does not include design changes or new functionalities.

- [ ] **12. "Do you have a written contract or agreement with the project scope, schedule, payment method, and responsibilities?"** Without a contract, there is no project. Period. The contract must specify what it includes, what it does NOT include, how many revisions you have per stage, delivery dates, and payment conditions. A common and fair scheme: 40% at the start, 30% upon design approval, 30% at launch.

- [ ] **13. "What happens if the project is delayed? Is there a penalty or adjustment?"** Clarify from the beginning what happens if the developer doesn't meet the agreed deadlines and vice versa: if you delay in sending content, the schedule also adjusts.

- [ ] **14. "Do you offer training so that I or my team can manage the site?"** Even if you have an administration panel, you need someone to explain how to use it. 1 or 2 hours of training should be included. Some developers record a tutorial video that you can consult later.

- [ ] **15. "How much does monthly maintenance cost and what does it include?"** Ask this BEFORE, not after. Typical maintenance includes: security updates, backups, uptime monitoring, and support for questions. If you don't ask before, it can appear as a surprise cost you didn't account for.

## Common mistakes small businesses make with their website

After developing websites for dozens of Colombian businesses, these are the mistakes I see repeating over and over again. Learn them so you don't fall into them.

### Mistake 1: Delegating the website to the nephew who "knows about computers"

Your nephew is probably very smart, but knowing how to use Canva or having taken an HTML course on YouTube is not the same as developing a professional site optimized for Google, fast, secure, and designed to convert visits into customers. Cheap ends up expensive, and free ends up very expensive when you have to redo the site from scratch 6 months later.

### Mistake 2: Not defining clear objectives

"I want a pretty website" is not an objective. "I want my website to generate at least 10 new clients per month through Google" is. Define what you expect to achieve and in what timeframe. If you don't know where you're going, any result will seem good, even if your site has gone 6 months without generating a single client.

### Mistake 3: Copying the competitor's website

Your site must reflect YOUR business, YOUR value proposition, YOUR clients, YOUR personality. Copying the structure and texts from the competition makes you look like a cheap copy and also penalizes your SEO (Google detects and punishes duplicate content). Be inspired, don't copy.

### Mistake 4: Ignoring mobile

If your site was designed on a desktop computer and "then we adapt it to mobile", the process is backwards. In Colombia, more than 85% of your visitors will enter from a mobile phone. Design must be mobile-first: first for the small screen, then adapted to desktop. I've seen beautiful sites on a 24-inch monitor that are unusable on a 6-inch phone. And guess where your real client searches from.

### Mistake 5: Not including clear calls to action

Your site must tell the visitor what to do. "Contact us" is weak. "Request your free quote in 2 minutes", "Schedule your no-cost evaluation appointment", "Order your delivery via WhatsApp now" are clear calls to action that guide the client toward the decision you want them to make. If your site doesn't have visible buttons with concrete actions, you're leaving money on the table.

### Mistake 6: Abandoning the site after publishing it

A website is not a billboard you put up and forget. It's a living asset that needs maintenance, updates, and fresh content. Google rewards sites that update frequently. An abandoned site loses positions, becomes vulnerable to hacks, and over time looks like a closed business.

### Mistake 7: Using only social media as digital presence

Instagram, Facebook, and TikTok are rented channels. They are not yours. The algorithm changes, your account can be hacked, the platform can become irrelevant (does anyone still use Google+?). Your website is the only digital space you control 100%. Social media is the bridge to your site, not the final destination.

## Basic SEO for small businesses: what you actually need to know

You don't need to be an SEO expert or hire a $5 million per month agency. With these fundamentals well implemented, your small business can start appearing on Google for the searches that matter to you.

### 1. Google My Business: the first thing, before the website

If your business serves at a physical location, claim your Google My Business profile TODAY. It's free and it's the most powerful local SEO tool that exists. When someone searches for "Colombian food restaurant near me" or "dentist in El Poblado", the "Local Pack" results (the map with 3 businesses) appear even before organic results.

Fill out EVERYTHING: hours, real photos, services, phone, categories, frequently asked questions. And ask your satisfied clients to leave a review. A business with 50 reviews and 4.5 stars will always appear before one with 3 reviews.

### 2. Local keywords: think like your customer

Your customer is not searching for "professional public accounting services with emphasis on individuals". Your customer searches for "accountant in Bucaramanga for tax return" or "cheap accountant near me".

Make a list of 10 to 15 phrases your customers would type into Google to find you. Then make sure those phrases appear naturally in your website texts: in titles, service descriptions, blog, FAQs.

### 3. Content that answers real questions

The best SEO strategy for a small business is to answer the questions your customers are already asking on Google. Don't write about what interests you, write about what concerns your customers.

Examples:
- **Restaurant**: "What is the best Italian food restaurant in Chapinero?", "Where to eat bandeja paisa in Bogotá?"
- **Office**: "How much does a dental cleaning cost in Medellín?", "Does dental whitening hurt?"
- **Store**: "Where to buy cheap school supplies in Cali?", "Stationery open Sunday near me"
- **Professional service**: "How to file taxes if I'm independent?", "DIAN fines for not filing on time?"

Every real question is an opportunity to appear on Google with an article from your blog.

### 4. Technical aspects you can't ignore

- **Your site must load fast**: on a mobile phone with mobile data, not on office WiFi with 100 megabits. Optimize images, use modern formats like WebP, and don't fill the site with heavy animations. Google penalizes slow sites on mobile.
- **Clean and descriptive URLs**: your services page should be yourdomain.com.co/services, not yourdomain.com.co/?page_id=437.
- **Each page must have a unique and descriptive title**: "Home - Doña Ceci Restaurant | Colombian food in Chapinero, Bogotá" is good. "Home" is terrible.
- **Your site must be indexed in Google Search Console**: it's free, and it tells you exactly which pages of your site appear on Google, what errors Google detects, and what searches are bringing traffic to your site. Install it from day one.

### 5. Local SEO: dominate your geographic area

If your business operates primarily in a city, sector, or neighborhood, focus on local SEO:
- Include your city and neighborhood name in titles, descriptions, and content
- Create specific pages or sections for each area if you cover several: "Dental services in El Poblado, Envigado, and Laureles"
- Make sure your name, address, and phone are CONSISTENT across the entire internet: website, Google My Business, social media, directories. An inconsistency (example: "Calle 127 #7-19" on Google and "Calle 127 # 7 - 19" on your site) can confuse Google and hurt your local ranking.

## Mobile-first: Why your site must work perfectly on mobile

This topic deserves its own section because it's the most costly and most common mistake in web development for small businesses in Colombia.

### The Colombian reality in numbers

- More than **85% of Google searches** from Colombia are done from a mobile phone.
- **65% of total web traffic** in Colombia is mobile.
- **40% of users** abandon a website if it doesn't load in 3 seconds.
- **60% of Colombians** use prepaid data plans, which means slower connections and more sensitivity to data consumption.
- **Mobile conversions** (purchases, reservations, forms) grew 24% in 2025 in Colombia.

### What "mobile-first" means in practice

Mobile-first is not "the site also works on mobile". It's designing and developing first for the smallest screen and then expanding to desktop, not the other way around.

A well-made mobile-first site:

- **Loads in less than 2 seconds** on an average Colombian 4G connection.
- **Has finger-sized buttons**: no tiny texts that force you to zoom. The minimum recommended size for interactive elements is 48x48 pixels.
- **Uses readable text without zoom**: 16 pixels or more for body text.
- **Eliminates unnecessary elements**: invasive pop-ups, heavy sliders, animations that only look nice on a 27-inch monitor.
- **Simplified navigation menu**: hamburger with the 4 or 5 most important options, not 15 links that don't fit on the screen.
- **Short forms**: asking for 10 fields in a form filled out from a phone is the perfect formula for no one to complete it. Name, phone, and message. If you need more data, ask for it later via WhatsApp.

### How to test if your site is really mobile-friendly

1. Open it on your phone. Not the developer's. Yours, with your mobile data.
2. Do you have to zoom to read something? If the answer is yes, it's not mobile-friendly.
3. Are the buttons easy to tap without making a mistake? If you tap "Send" and something else opens, the mobile design failed.
4. Can the phone number be tapped to call without copying and pasting? It should. It's the minimum.
5. Does the WhatsApp button open the conversation directly? If you have to look for the number, copy it, and paste it into WhatsApp, you just lost a customer.
6. Pass Google's official test: search "Mobile-Friendly Test Google" and analyze your URL. It's free and tells you exactly what mobile usability problems your site has.

## How to start today? A 7-step action plan

You already have the information. Now the important thing is to act. This 7-step plan takes you from "I have nothing" to "I have a professional digital presence".

### Step 1: Define your main objective (30 minutes)

Grab paper and pencil (or a note on your phone) and answer: if your website could only do ONE thing well, what would it be?

- Generate calls or WhatsApp messages?
- Schedule appointments automatically?
- Sell products online?
- Capture emails to send promotions?
- Show your menu, services, and location?

Choose ONE. The rest are secondary. A site that tries to do everything ends up doing nothing well.

### Step 2: Identify your site type and necessary functionalities (30 minutes)

Based on the examples you saw in the [Examples by type of small business](#examples) section, decide what type of site you need (landing page, corporate site, catalog, e-commerce) and make a list of must-have functionalities. The "nice to have" ones go on another list.

For greater precision, use the [interactive calculator](/calculadora). You select the site type, check the functionalities you need, and get an immediate estimate with no commitment.

### Step 3: Set a realistic budget (15 minutes)

With the [quick guide by business type](#costs) you saw above and the [price breakdown](/blog/how-much-website-colombia-2026), define an investment range you feel comfortable with. Remember to include recurring costs (domain, hosting) in your monthly budget.

You don't need the most expensive site. You need the right site for the current size and objectives of your business. You can always scale later.

### Step 4: Prepare your content (2-4 hours)

80% of web project delays come from lack of client content. Before looking for a developer, prepare:

- **Your business presentation text**: who you are, what you do, why you're different.
- **List of services or products**: with brief descriptions and prices (or ranges).
- **High-quality photos**: of your location, your products, your team, your work. If you don't have professional photos, hire a photographer for a half-day session. Investing $300,000 in photos that will make your site look professional is one of the best decisions you can make.
- **Contact details**: exact address, phone, WhatsApp, email, hours.
- **Testimonials from real clients**: ask 3-5 satisfied clients for their opinion with their name and photo.

### Step 5: Find and evaluate developers (1 week)

Use the [checklist of 15 questions](#checklist) to evaluate candidates. Compare at least 3 options. Don't go with the cheapest or assume the most expensive is the best. Look for the one who:

- Understands your type of business (or is willing to understand it).
- Communicates clearly, without unnecessary jargon.
- Has a verifiable portfolio with sites you can visit and test.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a .com.co domain or can I use a free one?

You need your own domain. Period. A free domain like yourbusiness.wordpress.com or yourbusiness.wixsite.com screams "improvised business" and destroys customer confidence before they read a single word of your site. A .com.co domain costs between $40,000 and $80,000 Colombian pesos per year. It's the cheapest and most important thing in your entire digital presence. Buy it in your name, don't let the developer register it in their name.

### Is it better to have a site built with WordPress or one custom-made?

It depends on your budget and needs. WordPress with a good theme and well-configured plugins is an excellent option for most small businesses: it's more economical, faster to develop, has an easy-to-use administration panel, and has thousands of plugins to add functionalities. The downside is that it can be slower and more vulnerable if not kept updated.

A custom-made site with modern technologies (Next.js, Astro, React) is faster, more secure, and more scalable. The initial investment is higher but the performance and customization are superior. For a small business that's just starting out, WordPress is usually the most sensible option. For a business that critically depends on its website (e-commerce, reservation platform, SaaS), custom development is worth every peso.

### How long until I start seeing results after publishing my site?

Organic traffic from Google is not immediate. A new site can take between 3 and 6 months to start appearing for relevant keywords. However, you can start seeing immediate results if:

- You invest in Google Ads pointing to your website.
- You share the site on your social media and WhatsApp groups.
- You put the link to your site in your Google My Business profile (this accelerates local SEO).
- You ask your current clients to visit the site and leave reviews.

SEO is a medium-term investment. If you need results in the first few weeks, complement with paid advertising while organic traffic matures.

### What if I don't know anything about technology? Will I be able to manage the site?

Yes, as long as you choose the right technology. If your site is built on WordPress, you'll have an intuitive administration panel where you can add and edit text, images, products, and pages without touching a single line of code. It's similar to using a word processor.

If you opt for custom development, make sure the developer includes an administration panel (headless CMS like Strapi or Sanity) or a maintenance plan that covers the content updates you need.

In any case, demand at least 1 hour of training as part of the project. And record a video during the training so you can consult it later.

### Will my website work on all phones or only the most modern ones?

A professional site must work on any phone launched in the last 5 years. In Colombia, where many people use low-end and mid-range devices, this is especially important. The developer must test the site on at least 3 different types of devices (low-end Android, mid-range Android, iPhone) and in the most used browsers in Colombia: Chrome (dominates with more than 80% market share), followed by Samsung Internet and Safari.

Ask to see the site on a low-end Android phone before giving your final approval. If it works well there, it will work well on almost any device.

### Do I need a blog if I have a small business?

Yes, and it's one of the most underestimated marketing tools for small businesses. A blog allows you to:

- Appear on Google for questions your customers are asking TODAY.
- Demonstrate that you know what you're talking about (applies to any profession: an electrician who writes about "How to know if your electrical installation is safe" demonstrates expertise).
- Have content to share on social media and WhatsApp.
- Keep your site updated (Google loves sites with fresh content).

You don't need to publish 3 articles per week. One every 15 days already makes a difference. Start with 5 articles that answer the 5 most frequent questions your customers ask you.

Check out other articles on the [blog](/blog) to get inspired with topics and formats that work.
